The Business Question

"Can we tell a Lartisien Voyager traveller
when a property is low-demand, lower-priced,
and more available — from just hotel, month, and room type?"
Hotel Property
Which of the 24 Lartisien Collection ultra-luxury properties — Europe & worldwide?
Arrival Month
January through December — Christmas peaks, August school holidays
Room Category
Deluxe → Junior Suite → Suite → Grand Suite → Villa → Presidential
Why Does It Matter?
I travel frequently with the Lartisien luxury travel agency and always want to visit magical places during their off-season — saving money and avoiding overcrowded resorts. Off-season brings lower demand, lower prices, and a richer experience: staff can attend to guests more attentively due to lower occupancy, and the property can be enjoyed with less crowds, more exclusivity, and greater discretion.
Two ML tasks  ·  Classification (Premium vs Standard)  +  Regression (EUR/night)

Data & Feature Engineering

Dataset v2

  • 1,440 rows — 24 Lartisien-verified hotels, 6 room tiers, 10 sample dates
  • Preliminary EDA removed 2 IQR outliers (Cheval Blanc, The Brando) from 26 hotels
  • Missing values (hotel room count only) filled with per-hotel median, not mean — robust to skew/outliers in a small per-hotel sample, and this column isn't even used by the final model anyway
  • Target 1: is_premium (rate > €3,000/night)
  • Target 2: log_rate → back-transform via expm1()
  • 80/20 stratified split · StratifiedKFold(5) clf · KFold(5) reg
Leakage Prevention
All preprocessing inside scikit-learn Pipeline — StandardScaler + OHE fitted on X_train only. Cross-validation uses inner folds on train only. Test set untouched until final evaluation.

Feature Collinearity Check — VIF Audit

Critical Finding That Started This Redesign: Raw Month × Raw Days-to-Christmas, r = −0.998
The original raw features were near-perfect mirror images of each other — knowing the month told you days-to-Christmas almost exactly, and vice versa. Two features that are linear functions of one another add zero new information but do add multicollinearity, and neither captures the real shape of holiday demand (a straight day-count, not the sharp last-minute spike real bookings show).

Fix: both raw columns were dropped and replaced with non-linear engineered versions — "How Close to Christmas?" (a steep 1/(days+1) curve) and two cyclic "Which Month?" waves — which are no longer near-duplicates of each other (see the audit below: r drops from −0.998 to a manageable 0.503, and every VIF stays under the 10 threshold).

Why Do These Correlate? Real-Life Explanation
Which Month? ↔ How Close to Christmas? (r = 0.503)
Both peak in December. The "which month" wave naturally hits its maximum on 1 December; Christmas closeness also maxes out in December. They partially overlap in what they "see."

Why kept: Christmas closeness captures the sharp, nonlinear spike in the final days before 25 Dec — it rises steeply. The month wave captures the broader winter season curve (also high in November, early Jan). VIF = 1.45 and 1.83 — model can still separate their contributions cleanly.
How Close to Christmas? ↔ School Holiday (r = 0.331)
December is both close to Christmas and a school holiday month.

Why kept: school holidays also cover July and August (summer peak), which are far from Christmas. The two features measure entirely different demand drivers — Christmas surge vs. family travel season.
How Exclusive Is the Hotel? — VIF = 6.66, most elevated
This feature partially overlaps with resort type (e.g. island resorts tend to be the most exclusive tier). Acknowledged limitation. Still far better than 23 hotel OHE dummies (VIF >> 10). VIF < 10 = acceptable for tree-based models which are robust to moderate collinearity.

Methodology Recap — Leakage, Independence, Overfitting, Metrics

1 · No Data Leakage
Train/test split happens first (80/20, stratified). StandardScaler + OneHotEncoder live inside a scikit-learn Pipeline and are fitted on X_train only — the test set is mathematically invisible until the final scoring step, for every model in both ladders.
2 · Independent Features (VIF + Correlation)
All 7 engineered features were run through a correlation matrix and VIF audit. Every feature scored VIF < 10 (the standard threshold); the one elevated score (6.66) was investigated, explained, and kept deliberately — see the audit slide.
3 · How We Solved Overfitting
First, we tried to eliminate it directly: GradBoost was manually capped at max_depth=3 (regularisation); XGBoost's max_depth/n_estimators/learning_rate were found via a real GridSearchCV (12 combos × 5-fold CV) — which independently converged on the same values, validating the manual choice. Decision Tree's unconstrained gap (0.143) dropped to 0.06–0.07 for both.

Then, we recognised why the remaining risk matters less than it looks: this model only has to predict rates for the 24 known Lartisien Collection hotels, never an unseen brand — a narrower target than a general-purpose luxury-hotel model, which is part of why the small gap is credible.
4 · Choosing the Right Metric
Classification target is imbalanced (~35/65) — accuracy would flatter Dummy (66%), so F1-macro is primary. Regression: MAE (€1,677) is the most business-readable, same-unit-as-target metric and is robust to the few very expensive Presidential Suite outliers; R² is kept as the technical cross-model comparison figure.

Honest Limitations

Tier Generalisation Is Limited — Use Within This 24-Hotel Set
"How exclusive is the hotel?" has the highest VIF in the feature set (6.66) and each tier is built from very few hotels (10 / 8 / 6 hotels in tiers 1/2/3) — too small a sample to trust the tier score on a brand-new, unseen property. The train-test gap (0.06–0.07) was only ever measured on these 24 known hotels, never on a held-out new hotel. Because of this, the app should be read as valid for these 24 existing Lartisien Collection properties specifically, not as a general luxury-hotel pricing tool.
No Real-Time Rates
Flash sales, last-minute deals, and year-over-year price inflation are not captured. Cross-sectional historical data.
Decision Tree Overfits
Full Decision Tree: Train F1=1.000 vs Test F1=0.719 (gap=0.143). Capping GradBoost/XGBoost at max_depth=3 (gap=0.064) resolves this.
