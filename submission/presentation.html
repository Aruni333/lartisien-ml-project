What This Solves

"When is a Lartisien property quiet
and better priced — and by how much?"
Off-season isn't just cheaper — it's a different stay: lower occupancy means more attentive, less burdened staff, more privacy, more exclusivity. Pick a hotel and a month → get a tier (Premium/Standard) and an estimated rate, so the quiet season is easy to find.

Step 2 · EDA & Cleaning

Skew → Log Transform
  • Raw nightly rate: heavily right-skewed
  • Log transform → near-normal
  • Cleaner regression residuals + classifier boundary
Row-Level Checks — All Clean
  • No duplicates, no invalid rates
  • No inconsistent category spelling
  • Hand-assembled data, not scraped
2 Outlier Hotels Removed
  • IQR fence on hotel-level median rate
  • Cheval Blanc Randheli + The Brando — 2–3× portfolio
  • Left in → model memorises 2 brands, not pricing logic
  • 26 → 24 hotels

Step 3 · Feature Engineering + Selection

Type of Room How Exclusive Is the Hotel? Which Month? (×2 waves) How Close to Christmas? School Holiday? Resort Type
The Finding That Drove This Redesign
  • Raw month × raw days-to-Christmas: r = −0.998 — near-duplicates
  • Fix: nonlinear Christmas-proximity curve + cyclic month encoding → r = 0.503
  • Selection method: correlation filter + VIF audit
  • Every surviving feature under VIF=10, each with a clear business meaning

Step 6 · Benchmark & Honest Evaluation

Where Overfitting First Showed Up
  • Full-depth Decision Tree: Train F1=1.000, Test F1=0.719
  • Memorised training rows, not a real pattern
  • Depth-capped GradBoost/XGBoost: gap 0.14–0.39 → 0.06–0.07
Right Metric for This Data
  • Classes imbalanced (35/65) → F1-macro, not accuracy
  • Rates have expensive outliers → MAE, not just R²
Dummy stays in every leaderboard · every number here is test-set, never train

What Do These Numbers Actually Mean?

Classification — F1-macro = 0.805
  • Accuracy alone is misleading: guessing "Standard" every time already scores 66%
  • F1-macro forces the model to prove itself on both Premium and Standard, not just the easy majority
  • 0.805 = the model is genuinely good at telling the two apart, not riding a shortcut
ROC-AUC = 0.905
  • Plain-English: pick a random Premium booking and a random Standard one
  • The model ranks the Premium one higher 90.5% of the time
Regression — MAE = €1,677
  • Same units as the price itself — the most honest "how wrong is it, in euros" number
  • Estimate €3,500? Real price is usually within about €1,677 either side
  • Chosen over RMSE because a few very expensive suites don't distort it as much
R² = 0.736
  • 73.6% of why prices differ is explained by hotel, month, and room alone
  • The rest: real-world randomness — promotions, last-minute deals, demand spikes we don't have data on
